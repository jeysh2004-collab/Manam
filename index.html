    Self-check

    Two short questionnaires that doctors use worldwide: the PHQ-9 for low mood and the GAD-7 for anxiety. They take about two minutes each.

    These are screening tools, not a diagnosis. Only a doctor or mental health professional can diagnose depression or anxiety.

    Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by the following?

      Calm tools

      Small, practical exercises for hard moments. Use whichever one fits right now.

      Box breathing

      Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Slowing your breath tells your body it is safe.

      Ready4
      0 rounds

      5-4-3-2-1 grounding

      When your mind is racing or panicking, bring it back to the room you’re in.

      Thought reframe

      A worksheet from cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT). Write the thought down, then look at it like a fair friend would.

        For men: when “I’m fine” isn’t true

        Many men are taught to handle things alone. So stress often shows up as something else — anger, working nonstop, drinking more, or going quiet. Noticing it is the first step, and talking about it is a strength.

        These pages are for anyone. Read whatever fits you.

        Signs that are easy to miss

        • Getting angry or irritated quickly
        • Working all the time to avoid thinking
        • Drinking, smoking or gambling more
        • Pulling away from friends and family
        • Headaches, stomach or back pain with no clear cause
        • Sleeping much less or much more
        • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
        • Taking risks you normally wouldn’t
        • Feeling numb rather than sad
        • Feeling like a burden to others

        If several of these have lasted two weeks or more, take the self-check and consider talking to someone.

        Common pressure points

        Job and career

        Fear of layoffs, AI changing your work, long hours, or feeling stuck. Your worth is bigger than your job title.

        Money and being the provider

        EMIs, debt and supporting family. Writing down the actual numbers and making a plan often feels lighter than carrying the worry.

        Loneliness

        Many men lose close friendships after college or marriage. One regular call or meet-up with a friend makes a real difference.

        Becoming a father

        New fathers can also get depression and anxiety after a baby arrives. Feeling lost, irritable or disconnected is common, and help works.

        How to start the conversation

        You don’t need the perfect words. Something short and honest is enough:

        “I’ve not been myself lately. Can we talk sometime this week?”

        “Work has been really heavy on my mind. I don’t need advice — I just need to say it out loud.”

        “I’m struggling more than I’ve let on.”

        Talking side by side often feels easier than face to face — on a walk, a drive or over chai.

        Build a message to send

        If a friend seems off

        • Ask directly: “You’ve seemed stressed. How are you really doing?” Ask twice; the first answer is usually “fine”.
        • Listen more than you advise. You don’t have to fix it.
        • Ask about suicide if you’re worried. Asking does not put the idea in someone’s head; it often brings relief.
        • Follow up a few days later, and help them call a helpline or doctor if needed.

          Mood across life stages

          Periods

          Mild mood changes before a period are common. If the week before your period brings severe sadness, anger or anxiety that affects work or relationships (sometimes called PMDD), a doctor can help.

          After having a baby

          “Baby blues” — tearfulness and mood swings — are common in the first two weeks and usually pass. If low mood, anxiety or feeling disconnected from the baby lasts longer or is severe, it may be postpartum depression. It is common, it is not your fault, and it is treatable. Talk to your doctor.

          Perimenopause and menopause

          Changing hormones in your 40s and 50s can affect mood, sleep and anxiety. Mention mood symptoms to your doctor, not only physical ones.

          Burnout and caregiving

          Signs include feeling empty, snapping at people you love, and resting without feeling rested. Caring for children, elders or a sick family member while working is a heavy load.

          • Name one task you can hand over this week.
          • Protect one small, regular time that is only yours.
          • Say no to one thing without explaining at length.
          • Tell one person how you are actually doing.
          If someone at home is hurting or threatening you, it is not your fault. Call the Women Helpline 181, or 112 in an emergency.

          Asking for help

          “I can’t keep track of everything alone. Can we sit down this weekend and split things up?”

          “I’m more tired than I’ve been letting on, and I think I need to talk to someone.”

          Get help

          Reaching out is a strong thing to do. These services in India are free.

          Emergency 112

          If you or someone else is in immediate danger.

          Tele-MANAS (Government of India) 14416

          Free, 24/7 mental health support. You can choose your language. Also reachable on 1-800-891-4416.

          Women Helpline 181

          For women facing violence or abuse at home or elsewhere.

          Get help immediately if

          • You are thinking about ending your life or have a plan
          • You have hurt yourself or might hurt someone else
          • Someone is hearing or seeing things others don’t, or is very confused
          • Someone has stopped eating, drinking or getting out of bed for days

          Call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Stay with the person if it is safe.

          Finding regular support

          • Counsellor or psychologist: talking therapy for stress, low mood, anxiety and relationships.
          • Psychiatrist: a medical doctor who can diagnose and prescribe medicine when needed.
          • Government hospitals have psychiatry departments, and many districts run mental health clinics under the District Mental Health Programme.
          • Your family doctor is a good first step and can refer you.

          On your first call you can simply say: “I’ve been feeling low and anxious for a few weeks and I’d like to talk to someone.”

          Manam offers general information and self-help tools. It does not diagnose, treat or replace professional care. In an emergency call 112. For free mental health support anytime, call Tele-MANAS on 14416.

          You don’t have to go through this alone

          If you are thinking about ending your life or you are not safe right now, please contact someone now.

          • Emergency, if you are in danger112
          • Tele-MANAS: free, 24/7, talk to a counsellor14416

          If you can, tell someone near you how you feel and stay with them. Move away from anything you could use to hurt yourself.
